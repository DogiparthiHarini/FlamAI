# Part C — Casual-tone decision memo

**Recommendation: (b), a ≤1B inference-time rewriter — staged behind a Day-1 prompting probe.**

## Assumptions

1. "Main model" is our already-deployed assistant (~FLM-4B scale, per Part B); calling it for
   synthetic-data generation is internal compute, not an external API.
2. An open ≤1B multilingual base exists with *some* pretraining exposure to all six languages
   (e.g. Qwen2.5-0.5B). Unverified risk: its Bengali/Marathi/Telugu coverage may be weak.
3. Reviewer's 10h/week is real, schedulable, from week 1.
4. "Casual" is ratable consistently by one reviewer against a rubric we define on day 1 — not
   yet validated, which is exactly what the day-1 experiment checks first.

## Why (b), not (a) or (c)

The reviewer covers **2 of the 6 required languages** — Tamil/Telugu/Bengali/Marathi get
**zero** native verification this cycle. That, not model quality, is the binding constraint. It
argues against (a): an SFT pass changes the one model serving all six languages, is hardest of
the three to roll back, and is what we're least able to verify for 4/6 languages before a
3-week gate. A separate rewriter (b) decouples "sounds casual" from "is correct," can be
**feature-flagged per language** (on for Hindi+Kannada, off elsewhere until reviewed), and
disables in seconds if it misbehaves. (c) prompt-only is safest and free, but formality is
plausibly baked into these models' learned distributions for mid-resource languages (most Indic
instruction data is itself formal/translated — the same register bias we measured in FLORES for
Part A), so likely a real but insufficient fix alone. Hence: use (c) first to measure that
ceiling cheaply; commit GPU to (b) only if (c) undershoots.

## Back-of-envelope arithmetic

- **GPU isn't the bottleneck.** 1×A100-80GB × 2wk = 336 GPU-hours. ~3,000 synthetic pairs
  (500/language × 6), generated by the main model, is well under a GPU-day; training a ≤1B
  LoRA rewriter on that for a few epochs is a few more hours, repeatable ~10× for iteration in
  under a week — leaving most of the 2 weeks for eval and a serving-latency check (the rewriter
  adds a second forward pass/request — worth sizing with Part B's method before launch).
- **Reviewer is the real bottleneck:** 10h/wk × ~2.5 usable weeks ≈ 24h, Hindi+Kannada only. At
  ~1–2 min/rating that's ~700-1400 ratings: ~1-2h for 20-30 gold seed exemplars/language, the
  bulk for held-out eval (~100-150 prompts/language, 2 checkpoints), a reserve for final go/no-go.
- **Data volume:** ~500 pairs/language is reasonable for a narrow style-transfer skill (not new
  knowledge) on a ≤1B model — few-shot generated from the vetted Hindi/Kannada exemplars, with
  unverified cross-lingual transfer for the other four.

## Success metric

Per language, **100-prompt held-out eval, reviewer-rated: ≥70% "casual" (≥4/5 naturalness)** for
Hindi/Kannada, **AND ≤5% factual/safety regression** on a 30-prompt spot-check vs. the original
formal response (rewrite must not silently change what was said). For the four unreviewed
languages: ship only as "provisional, pending native review," gated on an automated proxy (main
model's own casual-vs-formal self-rating) being no worse than pre-launch baseline.

## Kill criterion

**By end of week 2:** if Hindi/Kannada naturalness is <50% after one full data/prompt
refinement iteration, abandon the rewriter for launch, ship (c) only, and push SFT/rewriter
work to the next cycle. **At any point:** if factual/safety regression exceeds 5%, kill
immediately regardless of style gains — sounding casual isn't worth being wrong.

## Day-1 experiment

Zero GPU: take the unmodified main model, write 3-5 casual-tone system-prompt variants,
generate outputs for ~20-30 fixed Hindi/Kannada prompts, reviewer-rate same day. This gives the
(c)-alone ceiling before spending GPU time, calibrates the reviewer's rubric against real
outputs (needed before the thresholds above mean anything), and either shows prompting already
suffices (saving the whole GPU budget) or clearly doesn't — in which case those same rated
outputs seed rewriter training data on day 2.
